Two screens and a key

Palestinian film weeks and concerts were announced on silkscreened posters: a few flat inks pulled by hand onto white stock. VPalestine prints its pages the same way. Film is pulled in one ink, music in the other, and wherever the two land on each other they make a third colour that belongs to both. Type and photographs go down last, in the key plate.

Film
Music

Drag the music screen. On white stock the inks are transparent, so fuchsia over yellow prints red (#D80E07) by itself; nobody picks the red, the press makes it. That is the whole identity: two subjects, one page, and a colour that only exists where they meet.

  • The stock is the light. White on the page is unprinted paper, never a card or a panel.
  • One screen per ink. An ink’s fields, type and halftone register together.
  • Ink fields bleed. They run to the edge of the sheet or into another ink; nothing floats with rounded corners.

The V is two strokes

The mark is the V of VPalestine, taken from Anybody’s own V and split into the two strokes a printer would cut as two screens. The film stroke always leans in from the left, the music stroke from the right, and the overprint is the keystone where they cross.

  • Never swap the inks: film is always the left stroke.

  • Never rotate or slant it.

  • Never print it in one hue other than the key.

  • Never pull the strokes apart; the overprint is the point.

VPalestine, in its own type

Palestine is Anybody at width 72 and weight 860, set at the V’s cap height with 70 units between them. The wordmark is drawn as outlines, so it never depends on the font loading. Clear space on every side is one V stroke.

Four inks, two stocks

The palette is a print run, not a set of brand colours. Yellow and fuchsia are the two screens, red is what they make together, and the key carries the words. By day the page is white stock; by night it is black stock, where the inks are printed opaque over a white underbase so they stay as bright.

Inks are never text colours: type is always key, or the stock knocked out of an ink. Inks own whole sheets; they are not accents.

Set to the measure

Poster type was chosen to fill the line. VPalestine does it with Anybody’s width axis: every title line is set, one by one, to the width of its sheet, from width 58 for long lines to 150 for short ones, so a sheet’s lines are equal in length and different in width. Besley, a Clarendon, carries the reading.

  • Bands The billing block: labels in Anybody 650 at width 120, values in Anybody 750, tabular figures.

Every photograph is a screen

Photographs are printed, not placed. Each one is screened at 45 degrees into round dots sized from the picture, over the ink of the sheet it sits on; shadows stop short of solid so the ink always shows between the dots. Archive pictures come back in the key plate alone, like a reissued poster. The screen is drawn in the browser at the reader’s resolution, so it stays sharp at any size.

Coarse screens for big sheets, fine screens for faces at small sizes. Without JavaScript the same grayscale plate is multiplied over its ink: a duotone, still in the world.

  • Never a film still or a poster. Only photographs we can credit; the caption says what, where and when.
  • Never colour. Colour comes from the ink, not the photograph.
  • Crop to the person. Every plate carries a focal point.

Rank is the size of the sheet

Hierarchy comes from the format a story is printed on, the way cinema posters come in sizes, not from type size alone.

  • One-sheet

    The lead. Solid ink, bleeds to the edge, the biggest halftone.

  • Sleeve

    A record. Square plate, music ink, overprints whatever it lands on.

  • Half-sheet

    A festival with a programme. Solid ink, dates listed.

  • Window card

    A single screening or opening. Key on stock, photo on its ink.

  • Lobby card

    A short notice. Key on stock, no photo needed.

  • Handbill

    Listings by day: the week, the dates ahead.

Solid, tint, outline

State is ink density, never a new colour. A confirmed date prints solid. Something expected — a release without a date, a result still to come — prints as a halftone tint of its ink until it is confirmed. A date that has passed or moved keeps only its outline.

  • Solid

    Confirmed. It Must Be Heaven, Thu 15 Oct, 20:00.

  • Tint

    Expected. London names its documentary winner on Sunday.

  • Outline

    Passed or moved. Saint Levant’s Los Angeles dates, now spring 2027.

The V strokes are the legend: the left stroke marks film, the right stroke music, the whole V both.

How the press moves

Motion is what a print shop does, done once. The first page of a visit is pulled screen by screen; after that the page stays still and only answers the reader.

How VPalestine writes

Facts first, the work first, every date with its place and its source. VPalestine follows films and records; it does not decorate them with war imagery or pity.

  • Name the work, then the event.

    Elia Suleiman: all four films, from tonight in Athens

    Palestinian cinema shines in Greece

  • Expected stays expected.

    Her third album has no date yet.

    Rasha Nahas’s new album is out this month

  • Local time, with the place.

    Thu 15 Oct, 20:00 Athens time, Trianon

    Tonight at 8

  • Credit what you show.

    Gaza seaport, 7 October 2017. Not a still from Thirteen in Gaza.
