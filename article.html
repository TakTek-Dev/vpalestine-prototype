Athens Palestine Film Festival presents Elia Suleiman A World in Silence four features · Trianon Cinema · Onassis Stegi · 15–31 October 2026 · Greek and English subtitles

The Athens Palestine Film Festival, now in its fifth year, opens at 20:00 tonight at the Trianon Cinema with Elia Suleiman’s It Must Be Heaven (2019), and Suleiman is listed to attend. The screening starts a retrospective the festival calls A World in Silence: all four of his features, each with Greek and English subtitles.

The Time That Remains (2009) follows on Friday at 21:00, with a Q&A with Suleiman afterwards. Divine Intervention (2002) plays on Sunday at 21:00, also at the Trianon. The run ends on 31 October at Onassis Stegi with Chronicle of a Disappearance (1996), his first feature.

Who he is

Suleiman was born in Nazareth in 1960. The festival’s biography says he lived in New York from 1981 to 1993 (other accounts give 1982), then moved to Jerusalem in 1994, where the European Commission entrusted him with setting up the Film and Media Department at Birzeit University. He sat on the Cannes jury in 2006 and is an artistic advisor to the Doha Film Institute. Among the honours the festival lists are the Prince Claus Award (2008), the European Achievement Award in World Cinema (2022) and the Heart of Sarajevo (2024).

The rest of the festival

APFF26 runs until 31 October at the Trianon, the National Museum of Contemporary Art (EMST) and Onassis Stegi, under the theme “Nothing comes from nothing”. It is presented by Dounias, the not-for-profit founded by its director, Carol Sansour.

Three short-film programmes, Palescope 1 to 3, play at the Trianon at 18:30 from Friday to Sunday. Alex Bakri’s Habibi Hussein, about Cinema Jenin’s last projectionist, screens on 23 October and Who Killed Alex Odeh? on 24 October. Two evenings, on 24 and 25 October, honour Mohammad Bakri, who died last December, with his family present.
