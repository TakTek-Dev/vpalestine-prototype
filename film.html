The Oscar road

Best International Feature, 99th Academy Awards.

Citizen Osama

Palestinian Culture Minister Imad Hamdan announced on 31 August that Ahmed Hassouna's 69-minute documentary 'Citizen Osama' would represent Palestine, on the recommendation of an independent committee; The National counts it as Palestine's 19th submission in the category since 2003. The film follows Osama Al-Ashi, a former art photographer documenting the war in northern Gaza while protecting his wife and newborn child, and premiered out of competition in Venice. The Academy's submission deadline was 30 September; the shortlist comes on 15 December 2026, nominations on 21 January 2027 and the ceremony on 14 March 2027 in Los Angeles.

Sources: The National 11 Oct

Who IsStill Alive

'Who Is Still Alive' ('Qui vit encore') is built from the testimonies of nine Palestinians who fled Gaza and redraw their neighbourhoods in chalk; it was filmed in South Africa and contains no bombardment or archive footage. It premiered in the Giornate degli Autori at Venice in 2025, won the Prix de Soleure at Solothurn, and is Switzerland's submission for Best International Feature at the 99th Oscars. Its UK premiere opens the London Palestine Film Festival at the Barbican on 13 November, followed by a talk with Wadimoff and producer Jawdat Khoudary, with a second screening on 14 November.

Sources: Grazia Middle East, Barbican, London Palestine Film Festival 2026 15 Oct, I Am Hip-Hop Magazine 9 Oct

Before the screens went dark

In 1937 the Alhambra in Jaffa was one of the Arab cinemas of Palestine’s coast. Alex Bakri’s Habibi Hussein, now on the festival circuit, comes at that loss from the projection box: he shot it in 2008–2010 while working on the NGO-backed reopening of Cinema Jenin, and turns to Hussein Darbi, who started there at twelve beside his uncle and was later sidelined by the project. The cinema was demolished seven years after it reopened, The New Arab reported.

Mohammad Bakri, of Jenin, Jenin, died on 24 December 2025, aged 72. Athens marks him on 25 October; London screens A Life in Cinema on 19 and 20 November, each followed by a conversation with his son, Saleh Bakri.

  4. 30Oct

    Watermelon Pictures opens 'Who Killed Alex Odeh?' in US cinemas on 30 October, starting at the Quad in New York.

    Jason Osder and William Lafi Youmans's 84-minute documentary re-investigates the October 1985 tripwire bombing in Santa Ana, California, that killed Palestinian-American teacher and activist Alex Odeh, a case never solved. It won Sundance's Special Jury Award for Journalistic Excellence and screened at the Berlinale. Watermelon Pictures, which took North American rights in June, opens it on 30 October at the Quad Cinema (New York), Laemmle Glendale and Uprising Theater (Chicago), then the Frida Cinema in Santa Ana (5-10 Nov), Hudson NY (6 Nov) and New Orleans (13 Nov). It also plays Boston (18 Oct), Athens (24 Oct), El Gouna and the LPFF UK premiere (14 Nov).

  6. 23Oct

    Alex Bakri's 'Habibi Hussein', about Cinema Jenin's last projectionist, screens in Athens (23 Oct), Boston (24 Oct) and at the Barbican (18 Nov).

    'Habibi Hussein' is Alex Bakri's debut feature, built from footage he shot in 2008-2010 while working as cinematographer on the NGO-backed reopening of Cinema Jenin. It turns the focus to projectionist Hussein Darbi, who began working at the cinema at 12 alongside his uncle, and questions the foreign project that later sidelined him; the cinema was demolished seven years after reopening, per The New Arab. It screens at the Athens Palestine Film Festival on 23 October, at Coolidge Corner Theatre for Boston PFF on 24 October and at the Barbican for LPFF on 18 November with a ScreenTalk.

  18. 11Oct

    'Citizen Osama' by Ahmed Hassouna is Palestine's entry for Best International Feature at the 99th Academy Awards; the 15-film shortlist is due 15 December.

    Palestinian Culture Minister Imad Hamdan announced on 31 August that Ahmed Hassouna's 69-minute documentary 'Citizen Osama' would represent Palestine, on the recommendation of an independent committee; The National counts it as Palestine's 19th submission in the category since 2003. The film follows Osama Al-Ashi, a former art photographer documenting the war in northern Gaza while protecting his wife and newborn child, and premiered out of competition in Venice. The Academy's submission deadline was 30 September; the shortlist comes on 15 December 2026, nominations on 21 January 2027 and the ceremony on 14 March 2027 in Los Angeles.

    Sources: The National 11 Oct

  19. 10Oct

    Muayad Alayan's 'Conversation With the Sea' moves from Venice and Toronto to Doha's competition (19-27 Nov) and closes the London Palestine Film Festival on 27 November.

    Muayad Alayan's 112-minute 'Conversation With the Sea', written with Rami Alayan and starring Kamel El Basha, world-premiered in Venice Spotlight on 7 September 2026 and had its North American premiere in TIFF's Centerpiece. It is a Palestine-Netherlands-Saudi Arabia-Qatar co-production; MAD World holds worldwide sales except Benelux. It makes its MENA premiere in the Doha Film Festival competition and is the closing film of the London Palestine Film Festival at Curzon Mayfair on 27 November.

    Rami Alayan, co-writer, at the Mostra de València, 21 October 2023. Photo: Francesc Fort / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

    Sources: La Biennale di Venezia 15 Oct, MAD World, Arab News 10 Oct

  20. 9Oct

    London Palestine Film Festival 2026 runs 13-27 November across a dozen London venues, from the Barbican to Curzon Mayfair.

    The London Palestine Film Festival returns 13-27 November 2026, opening at the Barbican with 'Who Is Still Alive' and closing on 27 November at Curzon Mayfair with 'Conversation with the Sea'. The programme includes the UK premiere of 'Who Killed Alex Odeh?' (14 Nov, ICA), 'Edward Said: Between Worlds' (15 Nov, Cine Lumiere; 22 Nov, Rio), 'From Ground Zero+' shorts from Gaza (16 Nov, Barbican), 'Habibi Hussein' (18 Nov, Barbican), 'Gaza's Twins, Come Back to Me' (22 Nov) and 'Concrete Land' (26 Nov, Bertha DocHouse). Other venues include Curzon Hoxton, Curzon Soho, Riverside Studios, The Garden Cinema, Sands Films and Ropetackle in Shoreham.

    Curzon Mayfair, London, June 2023. Photo: No Swan So Fine / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

    Sources: I Am Hip-Hop Magazine 9 Oct, Barbican, London Palestine Film Festival 2026 15 Oct

  21. 9Oct

    The 19th Toronto Palestine Film Festival ended its online window on 9 October after in-person screenings from 30 September to 4 October.

    The 19th Toronto Palestine Film Festival ran in person from 30 September to 4 October 2026, opening with 'Endless Sunlight' and Annemarie Jacir's 'Palestine 36' and closing with Abdallah Al-Khatib's 'Chronicles from the Siege'; selected films streamed on Eventive until 9 October. The programme included 'Who Killed Alex Odeh?', 'Yesterday the Eye Didn't Sleep', 'Edward Said: Between Worlds', 'Palestine Comedy Club', a 'Views from Gaza' shorts programme and a live show, 'TPFF presents: NARCY x Excentrik', on 3 October.

    Annemarie Jacir, whose Palestine 36 opened the festival, at TIFF 2025. Photo: Danielle G. Campbell / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

    Sources: Toronto Palestine Film Festival, Program 2026 15 Oct, Socialist Project event listing 15 Oct

  22. 8Oct

    Doha Film Festival named its 15-film international competition on 8 October; 'Thirteen in Gaza' and 'Conversation with the Sea' make their MENA premieres there, 19-27 November.

    The Doha Film Festival announced 15 films for its 2026 International Feature Film Competition on 8 October, 11 of them supported by Doha Film Institute grants and seven directed by women. Arab News confirms Hosam Abu Dan's 'Thirteen in Gaza' and Muayad Alayan's 'Conversation with the Sea' will make their Middle East and North Africa premieres in the competition. Trade coverage (Screen Daily, Turkiye Today) lists four Palestinian titles, adding Rabab Khamis's 'Sama' (world premiere) and Mohanad Yaqubi's 'Revolutionaries Never Die'. Prizes include USD 75,000 for best narrative and USD 50,000 for best documentary.

    Kamel El Basha, 23 October 2021. Photo: Ahmed Nader Mohamed / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

    Sources: Arab News 10 Oct, QNA 8 Oct, Qatar Tribune 8 Oct

  23. 22Sep

    'Thirteen in Gaza' by Hosam Abu Dan won TIFF 2026's NETPAC Award; it had its Western New York premiere on 12 October and heads to Doha in November.

    Hosam Abu Dan's 77-minute 'Thirteen in Gaza', described as the first fiction feature shot in Gaza in 12 years, won the NETPAC Award at the 51st Toronto International Film Festival, where it premiered in Centerpiece. It is produced by New Scene for Media Production with support from the Doha Film Institute and the Arab Fund for Arts and Culture; Watermelon Pictures handles North American distribution and MAD World world sales. The Buffalo International Film Festival screened it on 12 October at the North Park Theatre.

    Gaza seaport, 7 October 2017. Not a still from the film. Photo: Mohammed Masri / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

    Sources: Arab News 22 Sep, Buffalo International Film Festival 12 Oct

  24. 15Aug

    Mohanad Yaqubi's 'Revolutionaries Never Die', a letter to filmmaker Jocelyne Saab, won a Special Jury Prize at Locarno and is now in London and Doha.

    Mohanad Yaqubi's 80-minute documentary (Palestine/Belgium/Qatar) reframes footage shot by Lebanese director Jocelyne Saab across Palestine, Lebanon and Iran between 1973 and 1983, prompted by an invitation she sent him before her death. It won the Special Jury Prize CINE+OCS in Locarno's Cineasti del Presente competition in August 2026. It screened at the ICA for the BFI London Film Festival on 12 October and is listed in the Doha Film Festival competition (19-27 Nov).

    Mohanad Yaqubi at Locarno, 10 August 2026. Photo: Segolene Liger / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

    Sources: Business Doc Europe 15 Aug, ICA, LFF: Revolutionaries Never Die 15 Oct
