How we work

Six rules every page is printed by.

  1. The work leads. A film or a record, and the people who made it, come before the event around it.
  2. Film and music share one page. Two desks, one grammar: neither is a sub-section of the other, and where they meet the page prints both.
  3. Every date names its source. Each story carries the pages it was checked against, with their dates.
  4. Expected stays expected. A release without a date or a result still to come prints as a tint until it is confirmed.
  5. Local time, with the place. Times are the venue’s own; a reader in another city converts, not us.
  6. Palestinian work at full dignity. No war imagery as decoration and no pity framing; the work is the story.

Corrections

When we get something wrong, the story says so.

Corrections are printed at the foot of the story they correct, with the date of the change and what was wrong. A changed date or venue is marked on the listing itself: the old date keeps its outline, the new one prints in ink.
